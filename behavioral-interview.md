# The Anthropic behavioral and values interview

Anthropic assesses values directly, in a round that candidates call the culture and values interview. It is widely reported as the round where the most candidates fail, including engineers who pass every technical round. The same assessment begins earlier, in the written "Why Anthropic?" answer, and it continues in the hiring manager and second onsite conversations.

## Where values are assessed

| Stage | What is asked |
|---|---|
| Written application | Why you want to work at Anthropic, often in 200 to 400 words |
| Recruiter screen | The same question aloud, plus what you know about the company's approach to AI safety |
| Culture and values round | AI safety, risk, responsible deployment, and times your values were tested |
| Experiences and goals round | Collaboration, disagreement, difficult feedback, and what you want next |

Interviewers are reported to compare notes across the loop. How you handle a changed requirement in coding, or a challenge in system design, adds to the same assessment.

## What interviewers look for

Anthropic describes its culture as high-trust and low-ego. Sources name three kinds of evidence:

- **Intellectual honesty.** You say what you think, admit what you do not know, and describe failures plainly.
- **Considered views on AI.** "I have not thought about it" is reported as disqualifying.
- **Low-ego collaboration.** You credit others, take ownership outside your job description, and disagree directly.

## Question themes

| Theme | Example question |
|---|---|
| Mission and motivation | Why Anthropic and not another AI lab? |
| Values under pressure | Tell me about a time your values were tested at work. |
| Judgment and trade-offs | Tell me about a time you balanced speed against safety. |
| Disagreement | Describe a time you changed your mind about something important. |
| Ownership and humility | Tell me about a serious mistake and what you did afterward. |
| AI risk | What are the ethical risks of deploying agentic AI systems? |
| Critique | Do you have any critique of Anthropic's direction? |

An agentic AI system is one in which a model chooses and carries out actions with tools. The critique question is reported as sincere. Agreeing with everything is the weak answer. A specific, reasoned disagreement that you state calmly is stronger evidence than praise.

## The "Why Anthropic?" answer

A strong answer does three things:

1. It names one specific thing you have read or used, such as Claude, an interpretability result, or the Responsible Scaling Policy.
2. It connects that thing to your own work.
3. It states the skill you would contribute.

Delete any sentence that would still be true with another lab's name in it. Write an answer that you can also say aloud in about 90 seconds, because the question returns in later rounds.

## How to prepare stories

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. It is a four-part order for telling a work story. The structure still helps here, but candidates report that heavily scripted STAR stories are received worse than honest answers with reflection in them.

- Write six to eight real stories across the themes above. Include a disagreement, a mistake you owned, and a time you chose care over speed.
- For each story, add what you got wrong and what you learned.
- For trade-off stories, state what you weighed, what you were unsure about, and what signal would have changed your decision.
- Read Anthropic's published views on AI safety and its Responsible Scaling Policy. Note one point where you disagree.

Two failures are reported often. Uncritical enthusiasm suggests that you have not considered the risks. A rehearsed answer sounds emotionally flat. Changing your position when the interviewer gives a good argument is reported as the behavior under test.
