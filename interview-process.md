# The Anthropic interview process, stage by stage

The Anthropic software engineer process is commonly reported as a written application, a recruiter screen, a technical screen, a hiring manager conversation, and two onsite loops on separate days. Candidates report seven to nine rounds and a timeline from three weeks to a few months. The exact stages depend on the role and the level, so ask your recruiter which rounds your loop includes.

## The stages in order

| # | Stage | Commonly reported format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Written application | A form with a "Why Anthropic?" field; strong answers are often 200 to 400 words | Whether your motivation is specific |
| 2 | Recruiter screen | About 30 minutes on video, sometimes split into two calls | Role fit and spoken motivation |
| 3 | Technical screen | 60 to 90 minutes of live coding in a shared Python environment, or a timed assessment | Whether your code absorbs new requirements |
| 4 | Take-home, for some roles | A timed exercise done on your own schedule | Realistic engineering judgment |
| 5 | Hiring manager conversation | 45 to 60 minutes, sometimes with a code review | Engineering judgment and depth of involvement |
| 6 | First onsite loop | 2 or 3 interviews of about 60 minutes on one day | System design, coding, and culture and values |
| 7 | Second onsite loop | 2 or 3 interviews of about 60 minutes on a separate day | Experiences and goals, and a project review |
| 8 | References and team matching | Calls with one or more teams | Fit with one team, then the offer |

Interviews are reported to run remotely over video.

## The application and the two screens

The application form asks in writing why you want to work at Anthropic. The form is reported to state that the company values this answer highly. Candidates report that the same question is asked again in the recruiter call and in the values conversation. See [Behavioral interview](behavioral-interview.md).

The recruiter covers your background, the role you want, and what you know about the company's approach to AI safety.

The technical screen is described in [Coding interview](coding-interview.md). The hiring manager then asks how you made decisions on past projects and how deep your involvement was. Sources describe this conversation as the first real assessment of values.

## The two onsite loops

The first loop usually holds system design, a second coding round, and the culture and values conversation. The second loop is reported to be scheduled only if the first one goes well. It holds two rounds, and neither is a coding exercise:

- **Experiences and goals.** Someone at manager level or above asks about collaboration, disagreement, difficult feedback, and what you want next.
- **Technical project review.** You present one project that you owned from start to finish, often in a shared document. Interviewers ask what you chose, what you rejected, what broke, and what the numbers were.

## After the loops

If the loops go well, you speak with one or more teams, and references follow. Candidates report waits of several weeks at this stage while a team opens capacity.

Anthropic is reported to send rejection emails at every stage. The company states that it does not give feedback on applications or interviews. Ask the recruiter for a timeline at the end of each stage.

## The rules on AI assistance

Anthropic publishes guidance for candidates, and the rule is reported to differ by stage. Older advice says that no AI assistance is allowed anywhere. Sources report that the company has since changed that policy.

| Stage | Is Claude allowed? |
|---|---|
| Written application | Yes: write the first draft yourself, then refine it |
| Interview preparation | Yes |
| Take-home assessments | No, unless the instructions say otherwise |
| Live interviews | No, unless the interviewer says otherwise |

The guidance is updated, so follow the instructions your recruiter sends over any article, including this one.
